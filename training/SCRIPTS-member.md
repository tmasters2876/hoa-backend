# Training scripts — member set (for review)

The **narration** is what the voice says and what the captions show, word for word. The *italic* lines are what the screen does meanwhile. Edit any narration line; the video follows the script.

## 00-getting-started — Getting started

*Signed in as dev-member · about 3 minutes · 6 scenes*

**1. Sign-in**  
Welcome to the Plantation Lakes committee console. This short series walks through each part of it, one page at a time. This first video covers signing in and finding your way around.
*zoom: the sign-in card*

**2. Sign-in**  
You sign in with the username you were given and your password. If you were sent a temporary password, the console asks you to choose your own the first time you sign in, and that's it.
*screen: type username and password, sign in · zoom: the two fields, then the Sign in button*

**3. Sign-in**  
If you ever forget your password, the link under the sign-in button sends a reset link to the email on your account. Type your username or your email, whichever you remember. The link works for one hour.
*screen: show the Forgot your password link, then return · zoom: the Forgot your password link*

**4. Clauses**  
Once you're in, everything lives in the sidebar on the left. Clauses is the governing documents, laid out one rule at a time. Search lets you ask a question in plain English. Revision Flags is where the committee's work happens. Guide is the written walkthrough of the whole process, and Account is where your email and notification settings live.
*zoom: the sidebar, one item at a time*

**5. Clauses**  
At the top of the Clauses page there's a panel called Help and Reference. It opens and closes with a click, and it describes every feature on every page. When you're not sure what something does, that's the quickest place to look.
*screen: open Help & Reference, scroll briefly, close it · zoom: the Help & Reference heading*

**6. Clauses**  
Two things worth knowing from the start. First, nothing you do here changes a governing document by itself. The committee proposes, the Board decides, the attorney reviews, and the community votes. Second, everything is on the record, which is a good thing: your work and your reasoning are kept exactly as you wrote them. The next video looks at the Clauses page.
*zoom: slow pull back to the full page*

## 01-clauses — Reading the governing documents

*Signed in as dev-member · about 3 minutes · 7 scenes*

**1. Clauses**  
The Clauses page is the governing documents, broken into pieces you can read one at a time. Each card is one clause: the document it comes from, the page, the citation, and a few tags that say what it's about.
*zoom: one clause card*

**2. Clauses**  
The Browse panel at the top filters the list. Type a keyword, like fence or shed, and the list narrows to clauses that mention it. You can also pick a single document, like the Declaration or the Builder Guidelines, or a tag.
*screen: type "fence" in Keyword, Apply Filters · zoom: the Keyword field, then the shorter list*

**3. Clauses**  
Each card has a Read clause link. Open it and you get two things: a plain-English summary, and the exact text as it appears in the recorded document. The summary is for orientation. The recorded text is what governs.
*screen: expand Read clause on one card · zoom: the summary, then the verbatim text*

**4. Clauses**  
View Source opens the recorded PDF in Google Drive at that document, so you can see the page for yourself. That's the same file residents open when the community chatbot cites a rule.
*screen: hover View Source · zoom: the View Source link*

**5. Clauses**  
The flag button on a card starts a revision flag for that clause. We'll spend a whole video on flags, but the idea is simple: if a clause deserves a closer look, flag it, and say why in a sentence or two.
*zoom: the Flag button*

**6. Clause page**  
Clicking a clause ID opens that clause's own page. It has everything about the one clause in one place: the full text, the source link, and every flag anyone has raised on it. It's the best place to read before you comment or propose.
*screen: click a clause ID badge · zoom: the clause header, then the Revision Flags section*

**7. Clauses**  
One more thing. Export CSV gives you the whole set of clauses as a spreadsheet, if you'd rather read on your own. Next: asking the documents a question with Search.
*zoom: the Export CSV panel*

## 02-search — Asking a question

*Signed in as dev-member · about 2 minutes · 6 scenes*

**1. Search**  
Search is the quickest way into the documents. Ask a question the way a neighbor would ask it, and the console answers from the governing documents, with citations.
*zoom: the Ask a Question box*

**2. Search**  
Let's try one. Can I build a fence with T-posts?
*screen: type the question, click Ask · zoom: the question, then the Ask button*

**3. Search**  
The answer comes back in plain English, and every claim in it points to a clause. Here it says T-posts aren't named in the approved materials, so they fall under "Other as approved by the ARC" and need ARC approval. The citation goes to page thirteen of the Builder Guidelines.
*zoom: the answer, then the citation*

**4. Search**  
Below the answer are the clauses it relied on. Each one links to its clause page, so you can read the exact text behind the answer.
*zoom: the cited clauses list*

**5. Search**  
This is also a good way to find things to flag. If an answer surprises you, or two rules seem to pull in different directions, you can flag right from here. A topic flag ties the whole question, and the clauses behind it, to one discussion.
*screen: open the topic flag dialog, show the notes box, cancel · zoom: the flag dialog*

**6. Search**  
The same answers power the community's chatbot, so when the committee improves the documents, the answers residents get improve with them. Next: the Revision Flags page.
*zoom: slow pull back*

## 03-flags-list — The Revision Flags page

*Signed in as dev-member · about 1 minute · 4 scenes*

**1. Revision Flags**  
Revision Flags is where the committee's work lives. Every flag is one clause, or one topic, that someone thought deserved a closer look.
*zoom: the page heading*

**2. Revision Flags**  
The four tiles across the top tell you where things stand at a glance. Open means nobody has weighed in yet. In Discussion means the conversation has started. Awaiting Board means the committee has agreed and sent it on. Resolved covers everything that has reached an end, one way or another.
*zoom: each tile in turn*

**3. Revision Flags**  
The filters narrow the list. You can show only active flags, only the ones the Board has decided, or only clause flags versus topic flags. Active is the view you'll use most.
*screen: open the status filter, show the options · zoom: the filter row*

**4. Revision Flags**  
Each row shows the clause or topic, who raised it, its status, and when it last moved. Click any row to open the flag. The next video is about what you can do once you're there.
*screen: click the roof framing flag · zoom: the row, then follow onto the flag page*

## 04-flag-page — Working a flag, start to finish

*Signed in as dev-member · about 3 minutes · 8 scenes*

**1. Flag detail**  
This is a flag. At the top, the strip shows where it is in the process, from Open through the Board's decision. Below that are the notes from whoever raised it, and the clause it points to, with the recorded text and a link to the source.
*zoom: the lifecycle strip, then the notes, then the clause*

**2. Flag detail**  
Everything starts with a comment. Read the clause, read the notes, and say what you think. The first comment moves the flag into discussion on its own. Nobody has to change a status by hand.
*screen: type a comment, Post Comment · zoom: the comment box, then the strip moving to In Discussion*

**3. Flag detail**  
Talk it out in the thread. Comments are kept in order and stay on the record, which means the Board can see how the committee reached its view, not just the result.
*zoom: the comment thread*

**4. Flag detail**  
When the committee has language in mind, record it in the proposal panel. Current language is the text being replaced. Proposed language is the committee's version. The third box says where it came from, or simply "new drafting." Any member can save it, and it can be revised as the discussion goes on.
*screen: open the proposal panel, show the three boxes · zoom: Current language, Proposed language, Where the language comes from*

**5. Flag detail**  
A discussion ends one of two ways. If the committee agrees on the proposal, any member sends it to the Board. If the committee decides the clause is fine as it is, any member closes it with a short reason. Both are in the Committee actions panel.
*zoom: Send to the Board, then Close — no change needed*

**6. Flag detail**  
Sent something too soon? Recall from Board brings it back to the committee until the Board has decided, and the proposal unlocks again. Closed something by accident? Reopen picks it up right where it was. We're adults; mistakes are a click to undo.
*zoom: Recall from Board, then Reopen*

**7. Flag detail**  
After the Board decides, the outcome shows at the top of the flag. Board Approved means the proposal goes on to the community attorney and then to the owners for a vote. If the Board says not yet, the reason is written out, and the flag can be reopened and revised.
*zoom: the outcome box*

**8. Flag detail**  
That's the whole loop: flag, discuss, propose, send to the Board. Everything you type stays exactly as you typed it. Next: the Guide.
*zoom: slow pull back*

## 05-guide — The Guide and the path to a vote

*Signed in as dev-member · about 1 minute · 4 scenes*

**1. Guide**  
The Guide is the written version of everything in these videos, kept in the console so it's always the current version. If you'd rather read than watch, start here.
*zoom: the Guide heading*

**2. Guide**  
The diagram shows the whole path. The committee lane is review, flag, discuss, propose. The Board lane is approve or not yet. And the community lane is the attorney's review and the owners' vote. Nothing changes in a governing document until the owners have voted on it.
*zoom: each lane of the diagram in turn*

**3. Guide**  
Below the diagram, each step has its own section with the details: what to look for when you review, how to write a flag that's easy to act on, and how to track a flag after it leaves the committee.
*screen: scroll through the step headings · zoom: the step headings*

**4. Guide**  
Keep the Guide open in another tab while you work, especially the first few times. Next: your account and notifications.

## 06-account-and-notifications — Your account and email notifications

*Signed in as dev-member · about 2 minutes · 6 scenes*

**1. Account**  
Account is the last item in the sidebar. It's where your email and your notification settings live, and where you change your password.
*zoom: the Account page heading*

**2. Account**  
Put your email on your account first. It does two things. It lets you reset a forgotten password from the sign-in page, and it lets the console tell you when a flag you're part of moves.
*screen: type an email address · zoom: the Email field*

**3. Account**  
There are two switches. Flag activity covers new flags, and comments, proposals, decisions, and closures on flags you're part of. Board steps tells Board accounts when a flag is sent to the Board or recalled. Leave both on to hear about everything, or turn flag activity off to keep the address for password resets only.
*zoom: the two checkboxes*

**4. Account**  
Click Save. From then on, each email tells you who did what, where the flag stands now, the comment or reason, and a link straight to the flag. You also get a copy of anything you do yourself, as a receipt.
*screen: click Save · zoom: the Save button, then the confirmation*

**5. Account**  
Change My Password is just below. Enter your current password and the new one twice. Eight characters or more.
*screen: open Change My Password · zoom: the three fields*

**6. Account**  
That's the console. Six short videos, and you've seen all of it. If anything would make it easier to use, say so. The tool changes when the committee asks.
*zoom: slow pull back to the full page*
